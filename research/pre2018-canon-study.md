# A bounded study of pre-2018 explanatory craft

Study date: 15 November 2026, Asia/Kolkata. Status: selected public passages inspected; no claim that whole books, complete archives, or audio performances were read or heard. The companion [source ledger](canon-sources.json) records access and date evidence. The [craft profile](../profiles/pre2018-canon-profile.yaml) contains operational suggestions. The [benchmark manifest](../examples/pre2018-benchmarks/manifest.json) contains separate, short evaluation passages.

## What the cutoff establishes

The selection concerns works originally published or delivered before 1 January 2018. Original publication, delivery, edition, transcription, web hosting, and retrieval dates are different fields. A current webpage can present an older work with later corrections. Where a hosting date is not printed, it is unknown; a footer or search engine date is not substituted for it.

Age is neither a quality score nor proof that a passage was written by a human. A concrete counterexample is Karpathy's 2015 article: it includes explicitly RNN-generated samples. Those samples, third-party quotations, comments, navigation, and later additions are excluded from profile evidence. Attribution on an author's, publisher's, or institution's site supports provenance, but it is not a forensic authorship certificate. [Karpathy's article](https://karpathy.github.io/2015/05/21/rnn-effectiveness/)

This is a selected English-language craft library, not a representative history of good writing. The requested authors are mostly male and belong to a narrow set of scientific and literary traditions. Their strengths do not make all of their prose, factual claims, cultural judgments, or advice suitable for reuse. The profile supplies craft options; the user's own authorized writing, audience, and factual constraints take precedence.

## Practical synthesis

The following are project design choices inferred from the readings, not rules attributed verbatim to every author.

1. **Preserve meaning first.** Keep assumptions, quantifiers, numbers, units, dates, citations, and uncertainty. If a stylish revision changes any of them, reject it.
2. **Name the thing and the action.** Prefer an exact term to a grand but vague substitute. Define necessary specialist vocabulary rather than banning it.
3. **Give each paragraph a job.** A paragraph may establish a problem, explain a mechanism, justify a step, report evidence, answer an objection, or limit a claim. Sentence order should make that job recoverable.
4. **Make the reasoning visible.** Join claims to reasons and evidence; state the condition under which the inference holds. An analogy supports understanding only when its correspondence and limits are recoverable.
5. **Earn warmth through attention.** Use a real reader question, a helpful example, or a relevant first-person observation supplied by the user. Never invent memory, experience, quotations, or human imperfections.
6. **Match the venue.** Speech can repeat a central phrase and signal turns. A paper may need precise definitions and compact notation. Informality, short sentences, repetition, and passive voice are options, not universal quality scores.
7. **Revise with evidence.** Check referents, paragraph progression, and preserved claims. For important work, ask an intended reader to explain the argument back; this study itself has not performed that reader test.

There is no target “human” sentence-length distribution, punctuation quota, detector score, or mandatory roughness. The selected texts differ too much for one to be justified. Their useful commonality is deliberate service to the task, not a shared surface fingerprint.

## Access limits and exclusions

- **Shannon, Creative Thinking:** the coeditor's bibliography identifies a ten-page Bell Laboratories typescript dated 20 March 1952. An academic-hosted transcription and a later mirror are readable, but the inspected transcription has visibly garbled wording and no verified typescript facsimile. Existence/date confidence is high; exact wording confidence is low. It is recorded as a provisional lead and excluded from profile derivation and stored quotations. [Bibliography, item 72](https://neilsloane.com/doc/shannonbib.html), [transcription](https://www1.ece.neu.edu/~naderi/Claude%20Shannon.html), [mirror with 2020 additions](https://rafamarino.com/misc/shannon/)
- **Nielsen:** the author-site chapter request failed with HTTP 502 in this run. No chapter was treated as read and no Nielsen-specific rule was derived. [Attempted chapter](https://neuralnetworksanddeeplearning.com/chap1.html)
- **Tao:** the current *On writing* page was opened, but it is a changing collection with present-day additions. No dated pre-2018 snapshot was established, so it is excluded from the pre-2018 profile. [Current page](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/advice-on-writing-papers/)
- **Books:** the complete works of Zinsser, Pinker, Sagan, and Booth/Colomb/Williams were not read. The source ledger distinguishes metadata, author discussion, and actual excerpts. A catalog or publisher blurb cannot substitute for reading a chapter.
- **Speech:** written transcripts support observations about wording and structure. Timing, vocal emphasis, gesture, and audience reception remain unassessed.
- **Holdout:** two entire source documents, Orwell's *Why I Write* (Summer 1946) and Kalam's US-India summit address (31 May 2006), are declared in the manifest and excluded from profile and engine-rule derivation. Each supplies one short excerpt, 16 and 24 words respectively. A curator inspected them to verify the excerpts. Authors overlap with the analysis set, and historical model exposure is unknown. No heldout tuning is allowed: freeze the profile and engine rules before evaluation, and replace holdouts before using evaluation feedback to develop a future version. No benchmark results, reader gains, or authorship-classification performance are claimed. [Orwell source](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/why-i-write/), [Kalam source](https://presidentofindia.nic.in/dr-apj-abdul-kalam/speeches/address-us-india-summit-education-research-and-technology-university)

## Use and evaluation boundary

The profile should help an editor make a better explanation while retaining the user's voice. It should not be used to impersonate these authors, manufacture autobiographical texture, sanitize factual uncertainty, or label text as human or machine written. Its suggestions remain hypotheses about useful craft until evaluated on authorized drafts and the intended audience.

The manifest offers meaning-preservation and register checks, with concise source excerpts and no stored full texts. Evaluation must report the actual task, draft, revision, reviewer, and failure cases. A well-formed profile and a valid JSON file establish neither writing quality nor a successful evaluation.
