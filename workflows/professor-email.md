# Professor outreach and internship email

Load email-profile.yaml and the shared personal/canon profiles. Personal email cadence is unverified; follow the user's current wording and respectful conventions.

1. Establish the recipient, purpose, actual reading status, real project/coursework overlap, and one concrete ask. Consult the supplied paper or authorized primary source if a research connection is claimed. Missing evidence stays missing; do not say “I read” because a title was supplied.
2. Build a ledger: reason for contact; relevant experience supported by notes; paper-specific observation if genuinely available; request; practical dates/attachments. Lock names, dates, project titles, links and experience claims.
3. Draft one message. Lead with why this recipient is relevant. Include only background needed to judge the request. Use a respectful salutation, concise body and appropriate sign-off. Keep a supplied clear Indian English expression. No flattery, invented relationship, or generic enthusiasm.
4. Compare every factual statement to the ledger. If required information is absent, ask a focused question or return a draft with conspicuous unfilled fields for review; never silently fill them.
5. Audit with --genre email, run preservation against a supplied draft, and do semantic review. Sending is a separate action requiring explicit authorization.

Acceptance: a reader can identify the real overlap and ask; no invented reading, achievement or connection. If the existing email is clear, leave it unchanged.

Example failure test: request claims a paper was read but notes say only its abstract was skimmed. Preserve the narrower truth; do not convert it into a full-paper claim.
